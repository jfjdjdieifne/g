## English narration (120 words; recorded duration: 48.98s)

Serbia accepted almost every demand in Austria-Hungary’s ultimatum. It refused one: Austrian officials taking part in the investigation on Serbian soil. Vienna declared war anyway.

That was July 28, 1914—exactly a month after Archduke Franz Ferdinand and Sophie were assassinated in Sarajevo. In the days that followed, Germany declared war on Russia, then France. On August 4, German forces invaded neutral Belgium. Britain declared war on Germany that night.

Thirty-seven days after the assassination, a crisis centered on Serbia had spread across Europe. Not through one automatic chain—but through choices: an ultimatum, mobilization, war plans, and decisions to cross borders. So what changed at each step? Follow the decisions, and watch a regional crisis become a world war.
