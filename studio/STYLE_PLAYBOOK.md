# Select a style; do not inherit the previous film

Executable style IDs and allowed materials remain in `youtube_documentary/engine/STYLE_REGISTRY.json`. This guide explains decisions; it does not create a second registry. All styles inherit USER_RULES: meaningful motion, causal clarity, connected scenes, English content, contextual sound and actual encoded review.

## Scene-first decision, not style presets

For each idea answer: what new thing should the viewer understand, what causes what, which exact material best shows it, what changes on screen, why the camera moves, how this continues from the previous scene, and how sound supports the reveal. The renderer is chosen after the idea; the easiest renderer must not choose the subject or reduce the story to tokens.

Do not put “cinematic”, “3D”, “motion” or “professional” in a plan as a substitute for describing the actual event and informative view. A side/cut-face view may explain separation better than an elaborate rear orbit. A genuine moving witness may carry a documentary beat better than any authored reconstruction. A map must make geographic understanding stronger, not just add a pin.

## Reference study

Consult relevant `youtube_documentary/engine/reference_lab/` breakdowns only after current policy. They preserve timestamp-grounded observations of specific films. The original films and contact sheets may need Drive recovery; a written breakdown alone is not a claim that this new agent has watched those films. Inspect the relevant actual material and note timestamps before relying on it. Do not begin an unauthorized full-film research study just to satisfy onboarding.

## No false upgrades

A technically passing encode is not an approved style. Attractive texturing does not cure a weak explanation. A positive comment about voice or execution does not approve the topic, visual foundation, entire film, publication rights or all future uses of a technique. Approval scope lives in REFERENCE_INDEX, not in a guessed adjective attached to a filename.
